# Dental Sensor Search Log

Date: 2026-10-12 (Europe/London).
Question: Which existing dental sensing technologies map to which conditions, and how could they supply DDT observations?

## Method

Targeted web searches using two search engines (System 2 for broad discovery; System 1 for an authoritative-source verification pass). Prioritize original studies, official manufacturers and regulatory documents. This was a preliminary landscape scan, not a preregistered or exhaustive systematic review. No database export, PRISMA count or full-text quality score was produced.

## Selection record

Retained 27 selected source records linked to 15 sensor categories. See the [source register](dental-sensors-landscape.md#source-register) for URLs, source types, access levels and supported claims.

Broad review discovery leads (not the primary evidence for inventory claims):

- https://www.nature.com/articles/s44328-026-00079-w
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8837049/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8406820/

Exclude unrelated search results, duplicate records of the same publication, marketing superlatives and unsupported claims of automatic disease diagnosis. The System 1 pass returned many unrelated results; only the relevant Florida Probe manufacturer and original-study records were retained from that pass. System 2 follow-up recovered relevant official device records.

## Unresolved checks

- Full-text study populations, reference standards, bias and performance.
- UK availability/regulatory authorization, costs and export/API capabilities.
- Broader coverage: OCT, mobility, salivary flow, volatile-gas and temperature-specific applications.
- Do not generalize in-vitro or feasibility findings to routine clinical use.

## Search outcome

Prepared an English source-backed inventory and condition mapping. Kept measured properties separate from disease interpretation and proposed ISO/ontology design choices.
